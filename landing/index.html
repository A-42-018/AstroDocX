    <the problem/>

    Earth Is Too Far to Call a Doctor

    Long-duration missions expose astronauts to space radiation, isolation and confinement, altered gravity and a hostile closed environment. Over months, these can bring immune changes, bone and muscle loss, cardiovascular events and behavioral health problems.

    Beyond the Moon, a message to Earth can take many minutes each way, with periods of no contact at all. The crew has to spot these changes in themselves — often without a doctor on the line — and decide what to do next.

    Our answer is running now: AstroDocX — detect, explain, act, log, and sync with Earth later. Open the Crew Console →

      <pipeline/>

      How It Works

      01

      Detect

      Sensors + daily check-in

      11 metrics (vitals, sleep, exercise, reaction time, mood, dose, cabin air) are compared with each astronaut’s own baseline. It only learns while nominal, so slow drift is not learned away.

      02

      Explain

      Plain-language alerts

      Each alert says what changed, by how much, against whose baseline and since when — e.g. “4.3σ above Pilot’s baseline, since D27 07:00 MET”. No black box.

      03

      Act

      Step-by-step action cards

      Checkable steps with progress and a Done button. The alert opens, escalates, eases and resolves on its own as values recover.

      04

      Log

      Offline health record

      Every reading, alert and completed step is stored on the device (IndexedDB), with no network needed after the first load.

      05

      Sync

      Delay-tolerant link

      An outbox waits for link windows (12-minute delay, blackouts handled), then sends. Export the log as CSV; the Ground View shows only what Earth has received.
