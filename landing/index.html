<the problem/>

Earth Is Too Far to Call a Doctor

Long-duration missions expose astronauts to space radiation, isolation and confinement, altered gravity and a hostile closed environment. Over months, these can bring immune changes, bone and muscle loss, cardiovascular events and behavioral health problems.

Beyond the Moon, a message to Earth can take many minutes each way, with periods of no contact at all. The crew has to spot these changes in themselves — often without a doctor on the line — and decide what to do next.

Our answer: AstroDocX — detect, explain, act, log, and sync with Earth later.

    <pipeline/>

    How It Works

    01

    Detect

    Sensors + daily check-in

    Vitals, sleep, exercise, dose and cabin air are compared with each astronaut’s own baseline.

    02

    Explain

    Plain-language alerts

    Each alert says what changed, by how much and since when — no black box.

    03

    Act

    Step-by-step action cards

    Countermeasures the crew can follow right away, without waiting for Earth.

    04

    Log

    Offline health record

    Every reading, alert and completed action is stored on board, even with no signal.

    05

    Sync

    Delay-tolerant link

    The record reaches flight surgeons on Earth whenever the link allows.

    Crew stays in control
